BUSINESS APPLICATION DOMAINS
============================

The altitude an app tile sits at, independent of industry. Notes for deciding
what belongs in the app switcher and what is only ever a section inside one.


THE TEST
--------

A domain earns a tile when it has its own noun.

  Operations  -> a job
  Compliance  -> a clause
  Workforce   -> a person
  Financial   -> a dollar
  Assets      -> a machine

If a candidate's records turn out to be another domain's records seen from a
new angle, it is a section or a report, not an app.


THE CANONICAL SET
-----------------

Operations        Running the work.
                  Scheduling, dispatch, field execution, daily records.

Financial         Money in and out.
                  Ledger, AP/AR, invoicing, budgets, payroll, reporting.

Workforce         The people.
                  Directory, hiring, onboarding, training, leave, performance.

Compliance        The rules.
                  Safety, risk, quality, environment, audit, policy.

Sales             Winning customers.
                  Pipeline, quotes, contracts, forecasting.

Marketing         Reaching them.
                  Campaigns, content, brand, events.

Service           Keeping them.
                  Tickets, SLAs, knowledge base, satisfaction.

Projects          Planned work with an end.
                  Plans, tasks, milestones, resourcing.

Supply Chain      What you buy.
                  Vendors, procurement, purchasing, logistics.

Assets            What you own.
                  Inventory, equipment, maintenance, fleet, facilities.

Communication     Talking.
                  Chat, email, meetings, announcements, intranet.

Knowledge         What you know.
                  Documents, wiki, records, templates, search.

Analytics         What it all says.
                  Dashboards, reports, forecasting, data.

Legal             Obligations.
                  Contracts, matters, IP, disputes, entities.

IT & Security     The systems.
                  Identity, access, devices, infrastructure, incidents.

Admin             The platform itself.
                  Org settings, users, roles, billing, integrations.


THE ONES THAT BEHAVE DIFFERENTLY
--------------------------------

Communication, Knowledge and Analytics are usually cross-cutting rather than
tiles. Every domain generates messages, documents and numbers. Giving them
their own app creates two homes for the same thing -- a document in Knowledge
and the same document attached to the record it belongs to. They become tiles
only when the artefact is the point rather than the byproduct: Slack is
Communication as a product, Notion is Knowledge as a product. Inside a business
platform they are almost always services the other domains call.

Admin is chrome, not an app. It belongs behind the org switcher.

That leaves roughly eleven that hold up as genuine tiles, and no business needs
all eleven. Most platforms ship four to seven and let the rest be sections.
Past about eight, the switcher stops being a map and becomes a directory you
have to read.


BRIESA TODAY
------------

Shipped:   Operations, Compliance, Workforce
Planned:   Commercial (project money -- cost, claims, variations, retentions)

Four of the eleven, and four of the six most commonly shipped. The standard
omissions for a delivery-side platform are Sales, Marketing and Service, which
only matter when you sell to many customers rather than win a few contracts.

HR tucks inside Workforce rather than becoming a tile: its subject is already a
person, so licences, inductions, medicals, onboarding and leave sit beside
People and Training. "Workforce & HR" can be the name on the pricing page
without being a second tile in the grid.

Financial does not tuck inside Workforce. Different noun (a project, not a
person) and different buyer (the commercial manager, not the HR manager).
Timesheets straddle and resolve as: capture in Operations, rates in Workforce,
cost in Commercial. Payroll itself is an integration, not a build.


LIVE QUESTIONS FOR BRIESA
-------------------------

Subcontractors    The clearest gap. There is a home for people, projects and
(Supply Chain)    sites, but none for a company you engage. Prequalification,
                  insurance certificates with expiry, SWMS review, licences,
                  scope packages, performance ratings, back-charges. Nothing in
                  the current model can hold it.

Plant & Fleet     Most likely graduation. Already a section in Operations and
(Assets)          at site level. Becomes an app with servicing schedules,
                  prestart checks, registrations, hire in/out, utilisation.
                  Worth it if customers are plant-heavy.

Tenders /         The only pre-award domain. Everything today assumes the work
Clients (Sales)   is won. Bids, EOIs, prequals you submit -- the mirror of
                  Subcontractors -- client contacts, pipeline. Feeds Operations
                  when a bid converts.

Design &          Revisions, RFIs, submittals, transmittals. The biggest single
Drawings          gap against Procore, and heavy: it makes you a document
                  control system as well as an HSEQ one.

Legal             Contracts, overlapping whatever Commercial becomes.

Suggested order after Commercial: Subcontractors, then Plant & Fleet, then
Tenders.


GRADUATIONS RATHER THAN NEW IDEAS
---------------------------------

Already sections that could become tiles given enough surface underneath:

  Quality       ITPs, hold and witness points, NCRs, defects, handover/O&M.
  Environment   Waste, water, EPA licences, emissions reporting. Worth
                watching given AU mandatory climate disclosure phasing in.
  Training      Actual courses and assessments rather than a record of them.


THINGS THAT LOOK LIKE DOMAINS AND ARE NOT
-----------------------------------------

  Insights      Reports on the others. Correct as a per-domain section; a
                global tile would be a dashboard with a scope picker.
  Automations   A form builder and approval chains are platform capability
                every domain uses.
  Incidents     Safety's records seen up close. A section.
  Timesheets    Straddles three domains. See above.


ADDING ONE
----------

A new domain is one entry in DOMAINS in src/lib/scope.ts plus its section
groups. Routes, the app switcher, search scoping and the collapsed rail all
pick it up without changes.

A domain is a path segment, not a hostname -- the subdomain is the workspace.
So a new domain needs no DNS, and its slug carries no naming constraint beyond
being URL-safe. The tenant slug is the one that is a DNS name.


WHY THE WORKSPACE IS THE SUBDOMAIN
----------------------------------

company-1.briesa.com/compliance/safety. The tenant is the origin and the app is
a path segment, which is the reverse of an earlier pass and the reverse for a
reason worth keeping written down.

The origin is a boundary the browser enforces. Cookies, localStorage,
IndexedDB, service workers and the blast radius of any injected script all stop
at it. So the question is not "what is the product's top-level partition" but
"what should be isolated from what", and for something sold to companies the
answer is one company from another -- never Operations from Compliance, which
are two navs over one company's records.

Per-org settings fall out of that for free. Anything scoped to the origin is
scoped to the workspace with no code at all: a cookie written on
company-1.briesa.com is unreachable from company-2.briesa.com, so pinned
sites, recent pages and the sidebar's segment are per-company without being
keyed by company. Settings that belong to the *person* rather than to the
workspace -- the theme, notification channels -- are the exception, and they
say so by carrying an explicit Domain= on the parent. The default is isolation
and sharing is the deliberate act, which is the right way round for a tenant
boundary.

On one host it ran the other way. Every per-workspace value carried its own org
key inside a shared cookie, every reader had to remember to index it, and
nothing but discipline stopped one company's state being read while looking at
another's.

Two costs, both accepted. The tenant slug becomes a DNS name, so a rename is a
redirect and wants a stable id behind it -- and companies rename more often than
products do. And unbounded hostnames need wildcard TLS plus host validation.

What it buys beyond isolation: switching app is a same-origin navigation the
router handles, which is the move people make all day; switching company is
the document load, which is the one they rarely make. And a tenant that owns
its origin can be given a custom domain later.
